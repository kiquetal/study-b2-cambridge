# Relative Clauses (B2 Upper Intermediate)

Relative clauses, also known as adjective clauses, give us more information about a noun or pronoun. They are introduced by relative pronouns (who, whom, whose, which, that) or relative adverbs (where, when, why).

---

## 1. Defining vs. Non-Defining Relative Clauses

### a) Defining Relative Clauses

*   **Purpose:** These clauses give essential information about the noun. Without them, the meaning of the sentence would be unclear or incomplete.
*   **Punctuation:** No commas are used.
*   **'That' vs. 'Which':** We can use 'that' or 'which' for things/animals. 'That' is generally preferred in informal English, but 'which' is also correct. For people, we use 'who' or 'that'.
*   **Omitting the Relative Pronoun:** If the relative pronoun is the **object** of the verb in the relative clause, it can often be omitted.

    *   **Examples:**
        *   *The man **who lives next door** is a doctor.* ('who' is the subject - cannot be omitted)
        *   *This is the book **which I told you about**.* (or *...the book **that I told you about***, or *...the book **I told you about***. 'which'/'that' are objects - can be omitted)
        *   *The car **that is parked outside** is mine.* (or *...which is parked outside*. 'that'/'which' is the subject - cannot be omitted)

### b) Non-Defining Relative Clauses

*   **Purpose:** These clauses give *extra*, non-essential information about the noun. The sentence still makes sense without the relative clause.
*   **Punctuation:** Always separated by commas.
*   **'That':** We **cannot** use 'that' in non-defining relative clauses. Only 'which' for things/animals, and 'who' for people.
*   **Omitting the Relative Pronoun:** The relative pronoun **cannot** be omitted in non-defining clauses.

    *   **Examples:**
        *   *My brother, **who lives in London**, is a lawyer.* (Extra information; 'who' cannot be omitted)
        *   *The Eiffel Tower, **which is in Paris**, is a famous landmark.* (Extra information; 'which' cannot be omitted)
        *   *Sarah, **whose car broke down**, was late for work.* ('whose' shows possession)

---

## 2. Relative Pronouns and Adverbs

*   **Who:** For people (subject or object)
    *   *The woman **who called** me was angry.* (subject)
    *   *The man **who I saw** was tall.* (object - often 'whom' in very formal English, but 'who' is common)
*   **Whom:** For people (object only). Very formal, usually replaced by 'who' or omitted.
    *   *The person **whom I spoke to** was very helpful.* (formal)
*   **Which:** For things, animals, or entire clauses (subject or object)
    *   *The dog, **which was barking loudly**, woke me up.* (subject)
    *   *I bought a new phone, **which I love**.* (object)
*   **That:** For people, things, animals (subject or object). Used only in defining relative clauses.
    *   *This is the book **that won an award**.* (subject)
    *   *He's the student **that I mentioned**.* (object)
*   **Whose:** To show possession for people, animals, or things.
    *   *I know a girl **whose father is a pilot**.* 
    *   *The house, **whose roof was damaged**, needed repairs.* 
*   **Where:** For places.
    *   *This is the park **where we used to play**.* 
*   **When:** For times.
    *   *I remember the day **when we first met**.* 
*   **Why:** For reasons.
    *   *Do you know the reason **why he left**?* 

---

## 3. Prepositions in Relative Clauses

*   Prepositions can come at the end of the clause or before the relative pronoun (more formal).

    *   **Informal:** *This is the friend **who I travel with**.* 
    *   **Formal:** *This is the friend **with whom I travel**.* (Note: 'whom' is often used in this formal structure)
    *   **Informal:** *That's the topic **which we were talking about**.* 
    *   **Formal:** *That's the topic **about which we were talking**.* 

---

## Important B2 Tips:

*   **'That' vs. 'Which' for Defining:** While both are often acceptable for things in defining clauses, sometimes 'that' feels more natural. However, remember **no 'that' in non-defining clauses!**
*   **Omitting Pronouns:** Only possible in defining clauses when the pronoun is the object. If it's the subject, it's essential.
*   **Commas are Crucial:** Misplacing or omitting commas can change the meaning of a sentence, especially between defining and non-defining clauses. It's a common B2 error.
*   **'Whose' for Possession:** Don't confuse 'whose' with 'who's' (who is / who has).
*   **Prepositions:** Be aware of both informal and formal structures. For B2 exams, understanding both is important, but being able to use the more common informal structure naturally is key.
