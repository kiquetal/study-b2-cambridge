# Causative Verbs and Verbs with Object + Infinitive (B2 Upper Intermediate English)

This section covers common B2-level structures where verbs are followed by an object and then an infinitive, or express a causative meaning (making or causing someone to do something).

---

## 1. Have got to / Haven't got to (Obligation/Necessity)

*   **Meaning:** Similar to "have to," expressing obligation or necessity. Often more informal and can imply an immediate or strong obligation.
*   **Structure:** `Subject + have/has got to + base form of verb`
*   **Usage:** Used mainly in present tense. For past or future, "have to" is generally used.
*   *Example (Positive):* I've **got to finish** this by tomorrow. (Informal, immediate necessity)
*   *Example (Negative):* You haven't **got to worry** about it. (No obligation/permission not to worry)
*   *Comparison with "have to":* "I have to go" is more general, "I've got to go" feels more urgent or personal.

---

## 2. Make + Object + Infinitive without 'to' (Causative - Force/Cause)

*   **Meaning:** To force or cause someone to do something.
*   **Structure:** `Subject + make + object + base form of verb (without 'to')`
*   *Example:* My parents **made me clean** my room. (They insisted/forced me)
*   *Example:* The sad story **made her cry**. (Caused her to cry)
*   *Passive Form:* In the passive, "make" is followed by "to + infinitive": "I **was made to clean** my room."

---

## 3. Get + Object + Infinitive with 'to' (Causative - Persuade/Arrange)

*   **Meaning:** To persuade someone to do something, or to arrange for something to be done by someone else.
*   **Structure:** `Subject + get + object + to + infinitive`
*   *Example:* I'll **get my assistant to call** you. (Arrange for the assistant to call)
*   *Example:* We finally **got him to agree** to our plan. (Persuaded him)
*   *Note:* When "get" is used in the causative with a thing as the object, it's often followed by a past participle: "I need to **get my car repaired**." (meaning "I need to arrange for my car to be repaired").

---

## 4. Order + Object + Infinitive with 'to' (Command/Instruction)

*   **Meaning:** To give a command or instruction to someone.
*   **Structure:** `Subject + order + object + to + infinitive`
*   *Example:* The teacher **ordered the students to open** their books.
*   *Example:* The general **ordered his troops to advance**.
*   *Note:* More formal than simply "tell someone to do something."

---

## 5. Let + Object + Infinitive without 'to' (Permit/Allow)

*   **Meaning:** To permit or allow someone to do something.
*   **Structure:** `Subject + let + object + base form of verb (without 'to')`
*   *Example:* My parents **let me stay** out late.
*   *Example:* Don't **let him trick** you.
*   *Passive Form:* "Let" is rarely used in the passive with this meaning. Instead, "allow" is used: "I **was allowed to stay** out late."

---

## 6. Allow + Object + Infinitive with 'to' (Permit/Allow)

*   **Meaning:** To permit or allow someone to do something (more formal than 'let').
*   **Structure:** `Subject + allow + object + to + infinitive`
*   *Example:* The school **allows students to use** their phones during breaks.
*   *Example:* Are you **allowed to park** here? (Passive structure)

---

**Key Differences & Pitfalls for B2:**

*   **'Make' vs. 'Get' vs. 'Let' vs. 'Allow'**:
    *   **Make:** Force/cause (without 'to' after object)
    *   **Get:** Persuade/arrange (with 'to' after object)
    *   **Let:** Permit (without 'to' after object)
    *   **Allow:** Permit (with 'to' after object)
*   **Infinitive with or without 'to'**: This is the most common pitfall. Remember `make` and `let` take the base form *without* 'to' after the object, while `get` and `allow` take `to + infinitive`.
*   **Passive Voice**: When `make` is in the passive, it *does* take 'to' (e.g., "was made to do").
