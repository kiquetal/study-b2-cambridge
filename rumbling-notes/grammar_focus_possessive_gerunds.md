# Grammar Focus: Possessive Forms with Gerunds

## When to use Possessive Forms before a Gerund

Use a possessive form (possessive adjective or possessive noun) directly before a gerund (-ing form of a verb used as a noun) when you want to specify **WHOSE ACTION** the gerund is describing.

This is particularly important when:
1.  The gerund is the **object of a verb** (e.g., *appreciate, consider, mind*).
2.  The gerund is the **object of a preposition** (e.g., *on, about, of, for*).

The key is to clarify that the action of the gerund belongs to the person or thing indicated by the possessive form, rather than the person/thing itself being the direct object.

### Forms to use before a Gerund:

*   **Possessive Adjectives/Determiners:**
    *   **my** (e.g., *my going*)
    *   **your** (e.g., *your helping*)
    *   **his** (e.g., *his leaving*)
    *   **her** (e.g., *her singing*)
    *   **its** (e.g., *its raining* - less common for actions, more for things)
    *   **our** (e.g., *our reviewing*)
    *   **their** (e.g., *their applying*)

*   **Possessive Nouns:**
    *   **John's** (e.g., *John's coming*)
    *   **the child's** (e.g., *the child's telling*)
    *   **the company's** (e.g., *the company's expanding*)

### Examples:

1.  **Correct:** "Do you consider **our having covered** all topics?"
    *   (Not: "Do you consider we having covered...") - We are considering *the action of our having covered*.
2.  **Correct:** "The manager insisted on **our reviewing** the report."
    *   (Not: "The manager insisted on us reviewing...") - The manager insisted on *the action of our reviewing*.
3.  **Correct:** "I appreciate **your helping** me."
    *   (Not: "I appreciate you helping me.") - I appreciate *the action of you helping*.
4.  **Correct:** "They couldn't believe **his winning** the lottery."
    *   (Not: "They couldn't believe him winning the lottery.") - They couldn't believe *the action of his winning*.
5.  **Correct:** "We talked about **John's moving** to London."
    *   (Not: "We talked about John moving to London.") - We talked about *the action of John moving*.
