# Verb Patterns: Verbs Followed by Gerunds (-ing)

## 'Consider' + Gerund

**Rule:** The verb `consider` is followed by the **-ing form (gerund)** of another verb.

**Structure:** `consider + verb-ing`

**Explanation:**
When `consider` is used to mean 'think about' or 'deliberate on' an action, the action itself acts as the object of the verb. In English, when an action functions as a noun in this way, we typically use the gerund form.

**Examples:**
*   "I am **considering moving** to a new city next year." (Correct)
*   (Incorrect: "I am considering to move...")

*   "Have you **considered taking** a gap year?" (Correct)
*   (Incorrect: "Have you considered to take...")

*   "She **considered telling** him the truth, but decided against it." (Correct)
*   (Incorrect: "She considered to tell...")

*   "We **considered buying** a new car, but our old one is still fine." (Correct)

## Other Common Verbs Followed by Gerunds (B2 Level)

Many other verbs in English are also followed by the gerund. This is a very common B2 grammar point. Here are some examples:

*   **Admit:** He admitted **stealing** the money.
*   **Avoid:** Try to avoid **making** mistakes.
*   **Deny:** She denied **knowing** anything about the plan.
*   **Enjoy:** I enjoy **reading** novels.
*   **Finish:** Have you finished **eating** yet?
*   **Imagine:** Can you imagine **living** without electricity?
*   **Keep (on):** Keep **practicing**, and you'll improve.
*   **Miss:** I miss **living** near the beach.
*   **Practice:** You need to practice **speaking** English every day.
*   **Recommend:** I recommend **visiting** the museum.
*   **Suggest:** I suggest **going** to the cinema.
*   **Stop:** Please stop **making** that noise!

## Your Personal Gotchas & Key Reminders (from practice!)

*   **Remember `consider + -ing`:** It's a common error to use the infinitive (`to + verb`) after `consider`. Always remember the gerund form!
