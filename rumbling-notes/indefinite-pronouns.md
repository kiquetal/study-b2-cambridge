# Indefinite Pronouns: Someone, Anyone, No one, Something, Anything, Nothing, etc. (B2 Upper Intermediate)

Indefinite pronouns refer to people, places, or things without specifying exactly who or what they are. They are crucial for natural English communication and often cause confusion due to their usage with positive, negative, and question forms.

***

## The `SOME-` Series (Someone, Somebody, Something, Somewhere)

### Usage:
1.  **Positive (Affirmative) Sentences:** Used in statements where something or someone exists, or when expressing an offer/request where a positive answer is expected.
    *   **Examples:**
        *   "I saw **someone** hiding in the bushes." (Positive statement)
        *   "There's **something** I need to tell you." (Positive statement)
        *   "Can I get you **something** to drink?" (Offer - expecting a 'yes')
        *   "Could **somebody** help me with this box?" (Request - expecting a 'yes')

2.  **Questions (for offers/requests or when expecting a positive answer):**
    *   **Examples:**
        *   "Would you like **something** to eat?" (Offer)
        *   "Did **someone** call while I was out?" (You suspect someone did, or it's a polite inquiry)

***

## The `ANY-` Series (Anyone, Anybody, Anything, Anywhere)

### Usage:
1.  **Negative Sentences:** Used after a negative verb (e.g., *don't, didn't, isn't, can't*).
    *   **Examples:**
        *   "I didn't see **anyone** I knew at the party." (Negative verb: *didn't see*)
        *   "She doesn't want **anything** to eat." (Negative verb: *doesn't want*)
        *   "There isn't **anywhere** to sit." (Negative verb: *isn't*)

2.  **Questions:** Most common in general questions when you don't know or don't expect a specific positive answer.
    *   **Examples:**
        *   "Did **anyone** call while I was out?" (General question, unsure of answer)
        *   "Is there **anything** I can do to help?" (General question)

3.  **Sentences with a Negative Meaning (without a negative verb):** Used after words like *never, hardly, without, rarely, scarcely*.
    *   **Examples:**
        *   "He hardly ever goes **anywhere**." (*hardly* implies negative)
        *   "She left the room without saying **anything**." (*without* implies negative)

4.  **`If` clauses:** Often used in conditional sentences.
    *   **Examples:**
        *   "If **anyone** calls, please take a message."
        *   "Let me know if you need **anything**."

***

## The `NO-` Series (No one, Nobody, Nothing, Nowhere)

### Usage:
1.  **Positive Sentences (with a negative meaning):** Used as the subject or object in a sentence when the verb is affirmative, but the overall meaning is negative. Remember: **do not use a double negative!** You cannot say "I didn't see nobody." It must be "I didn't see anybody" OR "I saw nobody."
    *   **Examples:**
        *   "**Nobody** came to the meeting." (Positive verb: *came*, negative meaning)
        *   "I have **nothing** to do." (Positive verb: *have*, negative meaning)
        *   "There was **nowhere** to park." (Positive verb: *was*, negative meaning)

2.  **As a short answer:**
    *   "What did you buy?" - "**Nothing**."

***

## The `EVERY-` Series (Everyone, Everybody, Everything, Everywhere)

### Usage:
1.  **Positive Statements:** Refers to all people, all things, or all places.
    *   **Examples:**
        *   "**Everyone** enjoyed the party."
        *   "He knows **everything** about computers."
        *   "I've looked **everywhere** for my keys."

***

## Summary Table for Quick Reference

| | People | Things | Places | Usage Context |
| :---------------- | :----- | :------ | :------ | :----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **SOME-** series | someone, somebody | something | somewhere | Positive statements, offers, requests, questions expecting 'yes' |
| **ANY-** series | anyone, anybody | anything | anywhere | Negative statements, questions, `if` clauses, sentences with negative meaning (e.g., *hardly, never, without*) |
| **NO-** series | no one, nobody | nothing | nowhere | Positive statements with a negative meaning (no double negatives!), short answers |
| **EVERY-** series | everyone, everybody | everything | everywhere | Positive statements (all people, things, places) |

***

## Important B2 Level Considerations:
*   **Formal vs. Informal:** `Somebody/Anybody/Nobody` are slightly more informal than `Someone/Anyone/No one`, but both are widely accepted.
*   **Agreement:** These pronouns are generally treated as **singular**, so they take a singular verb.
    *   **Example:** "**Everyone** *is* here." (NOT "Everyone *are* here.")
*   **Possessive:** Use apostrophe 's (e.g., `someone's`, `nobody's`).
*   **Reflexive:** Use `-self` pronouns (e.g., `himself`, `herself`, `themselves` if context allows, or `themselves` as a common singular choice).
    *   **Example:** "Did **anyone** hurt **themselves**?" (Commonly used, though formally `himself/herself` is sometimes preferred, `themselves` is very natural for a singular indefinite pronoun).
