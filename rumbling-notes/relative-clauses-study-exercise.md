# Relative Clauses - Study Exercises (B2 Upper Intermediate)

These exercises will help you practice and solidify your understanding of relative clauses. Pay close attention to punctuation, pronoun choice, and whether the pronoun can be omitted.

---

## Exercise 1: Identifying Defining vs. Non-Defining Clauses & Choosing the Right Pronoun

*   **Instructions:** Fill in the blanks with the correct relative pronoun (who, whom, whose, which, that, where, when, why). Add commas where necessary. If the pronoun can be omitted, write it in parentheses `( )`.

1.  This is the restaurant ______ we had our first date.
2.  My sister, ______ lives in Australia, is coming to visit next month.
3.  I don't like books ______ have sad endings.
4.  The man ______ car was stolen reported it to the police.
5.  She showed me the letter ______ she received from her pen pal.
6.  The reason ______ I'm late is that my bus broke down.
7.  Dr. Evans, ______ is a renowned scientist, will give a lecture tonight.
8.  Is that the student ______ you lent your notes to?
9.  My grandmother, ______ will be 80 next year, still goes swimming every day.
10. They visited the town ______ their grandparents were born.

## Exercise 3: Error Correction

*   **Instructions:** Correct the mistakes in the following sentences. There is one mistake in each sentence related to relative clauses (pronoun choice, omission, or punctuation).

1.  My best friend, that I've known since childhood, is moving to another city.
2.  The company which I work for offers great benefits.
3.  He complained about the noise, which made everyone angry.
4.  This is the park where children can play safely there.
5.  The artist, who works are very popular, is having an exhibition.

## Answer Key (Do not look until you have finished!)

### Exercise 1 Answers:

1.  This is the restaurant **where** we had our first date.
2.  My sister, **who** lives in Australia, is coming to visit next month.
3.  I don't like books **which/that** have sad endings.
4.  The man **whose** car was stolen reported it to the police.
5.  She showed me the letter **which/that ( )** she received from her pen pal.
6.  The reason **why** I'm late is that my bus broke down.
7.  Dr. Evans, **who** is a renowned scientist, will give a lecture tonight.
8.  Is that the student **who/whom ( )** you lent your notes to?
9.  My grandmother, **who** will be 80 next year, still goes swimming every day.
10. They visited the town **where** their grandparents were born.

### Exercise 2 Answers:

3.  Mr. Thompson is my English teacher **who** always encourages us to speak.
4.  The small cafe **where** we often meet serves delicious coffee.

## Your Personal Gotchas & Key Reminders (from practice!)

*   **Defining vs. Non-Defining with Common Nouns/Places:** Be careful when identifying defining vs. non-defining clauses, especially when the noun is a common noun (like 'cafe' or 'books') rather than a unique proper noun (like 'Mr. Thompson'). If the relative clause is essential to specify *which one* you are talking about, it's defining and needs no commas. (e.g., *The small cafe where we often meet serves delicious coffee.* vs. *The Cafe Royale, where we often meet, serves delicious coffee.*)
*   **Uncountable Nouns:** Remember that uncountable nouns like 'coffee' generally do not take an indefinite article ('a'/ 'an') unless you are referring to a specific portion or type (e.g., 'a coffee' meaning 'a cup of coffee', or 'two coffees' meaning 'two cups of coffee'). For general statements about the quality, use 'delicious coffee'.
*   **Capitalization:** Proper nouns (names, languages) should always be capitalized (e.g., Mr. Thompson, English).
*   **'Where' vs. 'Which' for Places:** Remember to use 'where' when referring to a location where an action takes place, rather than 'which' for describing the place as a thing.
